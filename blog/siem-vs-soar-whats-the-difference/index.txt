10:Tbf2,<p>Security teams are often presented with both SIEM and SOAR solutions. While these technologies complement each other, they serve fundamentally different functions.</p>
<h2>What Is SIEM?</h2>
<p><strong>Security Information and Event Management (SIEM)</strong> is a platform that collects, aggregates, and analyses security event data from across your entire IT environment in real time.</p>
<p>Key SIEM capabilities:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Log collection</strong> — Aggregating logs from servers, endpoints, firewalls, and applications</li>
<li><strong>Correlation</strong> — Identifying patterns and relationships across disparate events</li>
<li><strong>Alerting</strong> — Notifying security teams of suspicious activity</li>
<li><strong>Compliance reporting</strong> — Generating audit trails for regulatory requirements (PCI DSS, ISO 27001, etc.)</li>
<li><strong>Forensic investigation</strong> — Historical data search for incident analysis</li>
</ul>
<h2>What Is SOAR?</h2>
<p><strong>Security Orchestration, Automation and Response (SOAR)</strong> takes things a step further by automating the response to security incidents that SIEM (or other tools) detect.</p>
<p>Key SOAR capabilities:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Playbook automation</strong> — Codifying response procedures as automated workflows</li>
<li><strong>Tool integration</strong> — Connecting your entire security stack (EDR, firewall, ticketing)</li>
<li><strong>Case management</strong> — Tracking incidents from detection through resolution</li>
<li><strong>Metrics and reporting</strong> — Measuring SOC performance and mean time to respond (MTTR)</li>
</ul>
<h2>The Key Difference</h2>
<p>Think of it this way:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>SIEM = Detection and visibility</strong> — "Something suspicious is happening"</li>
<li><strong>SOAR = Response and automation</strong> — "Here's what we're doing about it automatically"</li>
</ul>
<p>A SIEM tells your team there's a problem. A SOAR helps your team — or automates — the response.</p>
<h2>Do You Need Both?</h2>
<p>For most organisations with a dedicated security operations function, <strong>yes</strong> — both add value and work better together.</p>
<p>The decision depends on:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Team size</strong> — Smaller teams benefit more from SOAR automation</li>
<li><strong>Alert volume</strong> — High-volume environments need automation to scale</li>
<li><strong>Maturity level</strong> — Start with SIEM visibility, then add SOAR automation as processes mature</li>
</ul>
